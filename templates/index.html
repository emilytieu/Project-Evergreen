What is Green Hydrogen?

Green hydrogen is hydrogen gas produced by using electricity from renewable resources to split water into hydrogen and oxygen in a process called electrolysis.

Key benefits

Why green hydrogen matters

Zero emissions

When produced via renewable electrolysis, green hydrogen has no carbon footprint — from production to combustion, only water is released.

Energy storage

Green hydrogen acts as a long-duration energy battery, storing excess renewable electricity for use when the sun isn't shining and the wind isn't blowing.

Solves intermittency

By converting surplus solar and wind power into storable hydrogen, it bridges the gap between variable renewable supply and constant industrial demand.

Production

How is green hydrogen produced?

Green hydrogen is made through electrolysis — passing an electric current through water (H₂O) to split it into hydrogen (H₂) and oxygen (O₂).

When that electricity comes from renewable sources like solar or wind, no carbon enters the equation. The result is clean fuel ready to store, transport, and use.

Challenges

What needs to be solved?

Green hydrogen is promising, but scaling it requires overcoming real technical and economic hurdles.

  • High production cost — Currently $5–6/kg, versus $1–2/kg for grey hydrogen. Cost must fall dramatically to compete.
  • Energy efficiency — Electrolysis converts only 70–80% of input electricity to hydrogen energy. Improving this is critical.
  • Infrastructure — Pipelines, storage tanks, and fueling stations need to be built at scale globally.

Technology

The green hydrogen supply chain

01
Renewable energy
Solar PV, wind turbines, or hydro generate the electricity feedstock.
02
Inverter / converter
AC power is converted to DC for the electrolyzer.
03
Electrolyser
PEM or alkaline cells split water into H₂ and O₂.
04
Compressor
Hydrogen is pressurized to 350–700 bar for storage.
05
Storage
Stored in tanks, underground caverns, or as liquid hydrogen.
06
Transportation
Delivered via pipeline, truck, ship, or as ammonia.
07
Point of use
Industry, fuel cells, power generation, or heating.
